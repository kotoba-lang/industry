# Ghost Hacker - Episode 1
## The Fear of God

The afternoon sun casts long shadows across our office, the one I run here along the Shibuya canal. Through the wide, floor-to-ceiling window, the water glints with an impossible blue, a shade I’ve only ever seen in pictures of Okinawa. A water bus glides by, silent and serene, a modern vessel on an ancient waterway. From my desk, I, Tamaki, watch it go, just another Tuesday in the city of water.

In the back of the spacious, open-plan office, Kaede is in her element. Her brow is furrowed in concentration as she stares at a complex array of monitors, her fingers flying across a keyboard. Data streams, spectral graphs, and lines of code are reflected in her glasses. She’s diving, as she calls it, swimming through the digital sea of information that connects our world to the next. She’s the best coder I know, our digital anchor.

"Looks like you've got a bite, Tamaki," she says, not turning around. Her voice is calm, professional, but I can hear the underlying hum of excitement.

I sigh, leaning back in my chair. "Another new client?"

As if to answer, a soft chime echoes through the office. It’s the front door.

From his corner, Hibiki doesn’t stir. He’s lost in his own world, headphones clamped over his ears, a faint, rhythmic beat bleeding out. He’s our sonic architect, the one who shapes the mood of our work with sound. He’s always on his own wavelength, but his instincts are unerring.

"I'll get it," I say, pushing myself up. "Someone's got to."

The man standing in the doorway is dressed in a simple, dark suit, but he’s anything but ordinary. He's a Ghost. His form is translucent, a shimmering projection of a young man caught between worlds. His light flickers, a nervous, unstable pulse, as if a war is raging within him. He looks lost, and deeply troubled.

Before I can even speak, Nee-Chan, my own personal ghost and partner, stirs from her perch on my shoulder. She’s usually no bigger than my hand, a tiny, fairy-like being of light. But now, she fractures. Her form shatters into a thousand pieces of colored light, like a stained-glass window exploding in slow motion. It’s her unique way of reading a client's emotional state, a bizarre but incredibly useful talent. The kaleidoscope of colors tells me everything I need to know: this Ghost is drowning in conflict, faith, and fear.

"Excuse me…" the Ghost begins, his voice a quiet murmur, like wind through dry leaves. He keeps his head down, but I can feel the desperation radiating from him. "My master… he is pushing God’s voice away."

I meet his gaze, my light purple eyes taking in his flickering form. I’ve met countless Ghosts in this line of work, each with their own story of pain and regret, but his aura feels different. The anguish is old, deep, and tangled with something sacred.

He continues, his voice trembling slightly. "He is a devout man, a man of faith. But recently… something happened. An event that has carved a deep chasm between him and his beliefs. He can no longer feel the connection he once cherished."

I nod slowly, my heart going out to him. This is what I do. I’m a Ghost Hacker. I heal the wounds of the spirit.

"I want to heal his spirit," the Ghost pleads, finally looking up. His eyes are pools of sorrow. "But I cannot reach him. He has closed himself off. So I ask you… please, hack me. Hack into my soul so that I may find a way to reach his."

I look into his eyes and see it all: the profound love for his master, the frustration of his own powerlessness, and the desperate hope that I can be the bridge between them.

"I understand," I say, my voice soft but firm. "For your master's sake, we'll start by untangling the knots in your own heart."

From the back, Kaede’s voice cuts through the quiet office. "Tamaki, be careful. I'm seeing a significant distortion in the target's data integrity. This hack won't be straightforward."

I give a slight nod to acknowledge her warning. At the same time, Hibiki removes his headphones. A moment later, a wave of quiet, ambient music flows from the office speakers, a gentle current of sound designed to calm and center.

"Frequência pesada," he murmurs in his native Portuguese. *A heavy frequency.* His intuition is, as always, spot on. The music is his tool, and he wields it with the precision of a surgeon.

Now, it’s my turn.

I focus my will, concentrating my energy at the tips of my fingers. A soft, purple light begins to glow, coalescing into shimmering particles of pure energy—photons. My photons. They drift from my fingertips, a slow, silent river flowing towards the Ghost.

"Just relax," I whisper. "Let it go."

The photons touch his translucent form and begin to seep inside, sinking into his being like rain into dry earth. His body starts to glow with the same faint, purple light, his flickering form slowly stabilizing under the gentle pressure of my energy.

And with that, I close my eyes and let my consciousness fall forward, diving headfirst into the information space of his soul.

My consciousness lands in a vast, echoing space. Before me stands a Gothic cathedral, its spires reaching for a digital sky I cannot see. It should be beautiful, a testament to faith and devotion. But it's a ruin.

This is the landscape of his heart.

The magnificent stained-glass windows, which should have told stories of saints and angels in vibrant color, are shattered, leaving gaping wounds in the stone walls. The grand altar at the far end of the nave has crumbled into a pile of rubble. This is what he carries inside him: a faith that lies in pieces.

As I take in the desolation, Nee-Chan flits past me, no longer a storm of colored fragments but a focused avatar of an angel made of pure, white light. She flies towards the ruined altar and begins her work, her small hands carefully, gently, clearing away the debris, piece by piece.

"Good girl," I murmur, a sense of pride welling up inside me.

I can feel Hibiki's music here, an echo from the physical world. The ambient, healing tones seep into the very fabric of this information space. As the music swells, the harsh, digital light within the cathedral softens, becoming warmer, kinder. The synesthesia of sound and light working in tandem is a beautiful sight, a digital symphony soothing a wounded soul.

I manifest my own avatar, a stylized version of myself, and walk towards the Ghost, who stands trembling in the center of the ruined nave.

"How do you feel?" I ask, my voice echoing slightly in the vastness of the cathedral.

He looks down at his hands, then around at the wreckage of his soul. "Lighter," he says, his voice filled with a quiet wonder. "I feel… lighter. As if all the broken pieces are finally starting to return to where they belong."

His voice is brighter than it was in the office. It's a good sign. The healing has begun.

As if responding to his words, a soft light begins to emanate from the very stones of the cathedral. In the real world, I know Kaede is watching this on her monitors, seeing his data streams stabilize, the chaotic spikes smoothing out into calm, steady waves. I can almost picture her giving a small, satisfied nod. I can imagine Hibiki smiling, too, knowing his music has hit its mark.

The Ghost himself begins to change. The desperate, flickering light is gone, replaced by a dignified, unwavering glow. He stands taller, his expression no longer one of anguish, but of quiet, profound conviction.

"I feel like I'm not alone anymore," he says, his voice strong and clear. "I understand now. If I can accept his weakness, if I can be his strength, then he… he can surely learn to believe again."

---

**Returning to the Real World**

The hack is complete. My consciousness slips back into my body, and I open my eyes. The Ghost is still before me, but he is transformed. He gives me a deep, respectful bow, his form now a steady, brilliant beacon of light.

"Thank you," he says, his voice resonating with gratitude. "Truly. I will continue to walk with him, for as long as he needs me."

I offer him a warm, reassuring smile. "Don't rush," I tell him. "Take it slow. And come back anytime. I'd like to hear how your master is doing."

With a final, grateful nod, he dissolves, his light fading gently from the room, leaving behind a sense of peace.

Nee-Chan, back in her tiny, fairy-like form, zips through the air and lands happily on my shoulder.

"Thank you!" she chirps. "Nee-Chan will keep working hard at her avatar part-time job!"

I can't help but laugh at her enthusiasm. Across the room, I see Kaede smiling faintly at her now-stable monitors, and Hibiki gives me a cool, understated thumbs-up.

Another connection healed. Another soul set back on its path.

My work as a Ghost Hacker is never done. And my day is just getting started.

**(End)**
